Black Holes

Where Gravity Becomes Extreme

What Is a Black Hole?

A black hole is a region of space where gravity is so strong that nothing, including light, can escape once it crosses a certain boundary.

Black holes can form when massive stars collapse under their own gravity. Scientists also study much larger black holes located at the centres of galaxies.

Event Horizon

The event horizon is the boundary surrounding a black hole. After an object crosses this boundary, it cannot return to the outside universe according to our current understanding of gravity.

Accretion Disk

Matter falling toward a black hole can form a rapidly rotating disk of extremely hot gas and dust called an accretion disk. This material can produce enormous amounts of radiation.

Types of Black Holes

  • Stellar-mass black holes — formed from massive stars.
  • Intermediate-mass black holes — larger than stellar black holes.
  • Supermassive black holes — found at the centres of many galaxies.
  • Primordial black holes — hypothetical black holes that may have formed in the early universe.

How Do We Detect Them?

Black holes themselves do not emit visible light. Scientists can detect them by observing their effects on nearby matter, stars and light. Gravitational waves produced by merging black holes also provide important evidence.

Interesting Facts

  • Light cannot escape from inside the event horizon.
  • Black holes can collide and merge.
  • They can influence the motion of nearby stars.
  • Some galaxies contain supermassive black holes at their centres.
  • Black holes can produce powerful effects on surrounding matter.
  • Scientists continue to study them using different types of observations.
