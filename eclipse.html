☀ Solar Eclipse

When the Moon stands between the Sun and Earth.

SUN
MOON
EARTH

ALIGNMENT: SUN → MOON → EARTH
The Moon blocks some or all of the Sun's light. Its shadow falls on a small area of Earth.

🌑

Total Eclipse

The Moon completely covers the Sun from the right viewing location, revealing the Sun's glowing corona.

🌘

Partial Eclipse

The Moon covers only part of the Sun, making it appear as if a bite has been taken out of it.

💍

Annular Eclipse

The Moon appears too small to cover the Sun completely, leaving a bright ring called the ring of fire.

🌞 Why does it happen?

A solar eclipse occurs around a new moon, when the Sun, Moon and Earth line up closely enough for the Moon's shadow to reach Earth. The Moon's orbit is tilted, so eclipses do not happen every month.

🌑 Lunar Eclipse

When Earth casts its shadow across the Moon.

SUN
EARTH
MOON

ALIGNMENT: SUN → EARTH → MOON
Earth moves between the Sun and Moon. Earth's shadow falls on the Moon, which can appear copper-red.

🔴

Total Lunar Eclipse

The entire Moon enters Earth's darkest shadow, called the umbra. It can turn a striking reddish colour.

🌗

Partial Lunar Eclipse

Only part of the Moon enters Earth's umbra, so a dark shadow visibly covers a portion of its surface.

🌕

Penumbral Eclipse

The Moon passes through Earth's faint outer shadow, producing a subtle dimming that can be difficult to notice.

🌍 Why does the Moon turn red?

During a total lunar eclipse, Earth's atmosphere scatters much of the blue light and bends some red and orange sunlight into Earth's shadow. This reddish light reaches the Moon, creating the beautiful Blood Moon effect.
