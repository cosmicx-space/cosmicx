What Is a Galaxy?

A galaxy is a massive system containing stars, planets, gas, dust and dark matter, all held together by gravity. Galaxies can contain millions, billions or even trillions of stars.

Our Solar System is located inside the Milky Way galaxy, which is one of the billions of galaxies in the observable universe.

Types of Galaxies

  • Spiral galaxies — have rotating arms extending from a central region.
  • Elliptical galaxies — have a smoother and more rounded appearance.
  • Irregular galaxies — do not have a clearly defined shape.
  • Lenticular galaxies — have a disk-like structure but less prominent spiral arms.

Our Milky Way

The Milky Way is a large spiral galaxy containing our Solar System. It has a central region surrounded by a disk of stars, gas and dust, along with a much larger halo of dark matter.

Supermassive Black Holes

Many large galaxies contain a supermassive black hole at their centre. The Milky Way, for example, contains a supermassive black hole known as Sagittarius A*.

Milky Way

Our home galaxy and a large spiral galaxy containing the Solar System.

Andromeda

A large spiral galaxy located relatively close to the Milky Way.

Triangulum

A spiral galaxy that belongs to the Local Group of galaxies.
