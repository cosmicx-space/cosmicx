Venus

The Hottest Planet in the Solar System

About Venus

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and is similar in size and structure to Earth. Because of these similarities, Venus is sometimes called Earth's planetary twin.

However, the two planets are very different. Venus has a thick atmosphere dominated by carbon dioxide and clouds made of sulfuric acid. This atmosphere traps enormous amounts of heat, making Venus the hottest planet in our Solar System.

Atmosphere & Surface

Venus is surrounded by a dense atmosphere that creates an extreme greenhouse effect. The pressure at its surface is roughly 90 times that of Earth's sea-level pressure.

Beneath the clouds lies a rocky surface containing mountains, large plains and thousands of volcanic features. Scientists believe that volcanic activity may have played an important role in shaping the planet.

Extreme Temperature

Venus has an average surface temperature of around 465°C. The thick atmosphere prevents heat from escaping efficiently, so temperatures remain extremely high even on the planet's night side.

Rotation & Revolution

Venus rotates very slowly and in the opposite direction to most planets in the Solar System. One rotation takes about 243 Earth days.

Interestingly, Venus completes one orbit around the Sun in about 225 Earth days. This means a Venusian day, measured by one rotation, is longer than its year.

Exploring Venus

Venus has been visited by numerous robotic spacecraft. Missions from different space agencies have studied its atmosphere, surface and geological history.

Because of the planet's extreme heat and atmospheric pressure, landing and operating spacecraft on the surface is extremely challenging.

Interesting Facts

  • Venus is the second planet from the Sun.
  • It is the hottest planet in our Solar System.
  • Venus rotates in the opposite direction to most planets.
  • Its atmosphere is mainly carbon dioxide.
  • Venus has no natural moon.
  • Its thick clouds contain sulfuric acid.
